Before you buy

Is CWT 5000 the right product for your tower?

Cooling tower chemistry comes down to one question: how hard is the water you're making up with? Get that wrong and you'll be fighting scale all summer. Here's the honest version.

✓ Use CWT 5000 if

  • Your makeup water runs through a softener — under about 1 grain (17 ppm) of hardness
  • You're treating an open recirculating tower or an evaporative condenser
  • You can feed continuously with a small chemical pump, or you're ready to add one
  • You'd rather run one inhibitor than juggle a scale product, a corrosion product and a dispersant

✕ Look at something else if

  • Your makeup is unsoftened city or well water — you want CWT 9000
  • You're treating a closed loop that doesn't evaporate — that's 558T
  • What you actually have is algae or bacteria — start with a biocide instead: BioBrom BT or DBNPA
  • It's a steam boiler, not a tower — see the boiler range

What it does

Three jobs, one drum

A cooling tower concentrates whatever is in your makeup water, over and over, as it evaporates. CWT 5000 handles what that concentration does to your equipment.

Keeps minerals in the water

A phosphonate and polymer package holds calcium and silica in solution instead of letting them bake onto your fill, tubes and heat exchanger surfaces — where a sixteenth of an inch of scale costs you real efficiency.

Films the metal

An azole component lays a protective film on copper and yellow metal, and the inhibitor package covers mild steel. That's the part you don't see working until you open the system up.

Proves the dose

A fluorescent PTSA tracer travels with the product. One meter reading tells you whether your pump is actually keeping up — no guessing, no waiting for a service visit to find out.

  1. Charge the system

    Pour the initial charge straight into the tower basin with the pump running. This gets you to strength in one shot instead of waiting a week for the feed pump to catch up.

  2. Set the feed pump

    Set the pump to the daily feed rate above. On a 30 GPD pump that's usually a low stroke setting — write down where you put it so you can tell if someone moves it.

    Read PTSA and conductivity. PTSA tells you if the product is at strength; conductivity tells you whether your bleed is holding the cycles you assumed.

Handling

What to know before the drum arrives

CWT 5000 is alkaline. It won't hurt you through a glove, but it will sting on skin and it does not belong anywhere near your eyes.

Wear the basics

Splash goggles and nitrile gloves whenever you're pouring or breaking a line. That's all this product asks for — no respirator, no suit.

Store above freezing

Keep it between 40 °F and 100 °F, sealed and out of the sun. It freezes around 28 °F; if it does, thaw it slowly and mix it well before feeding.

Never mix it in a line

Don't share a feed line or a day tank with acid or an oxidizing biocide. Separate injection points, and let the loop do the mixing.

The safety data sheet has the full hazard statements, first-aid measures and spill procedure. Print it and keep it where the drum lives — most inspectors ask for it.
